Spices of India

Cooking · 41 cards · names and uses · updated 2026-11-12

Get it on Google Play Add it in the web app

FrontBack
HaldiTurmeric. Earthy and golden, it colours curries and is added in small amounts.
JeeraCumin seeds. Warm and nutty, often fried first in hot oil.
DhaniaCoriander. Seeds are citrusy and mild; the fresh leaves finish a dish.
ElaichiGreen cardamom. Sweet and floral, used in chai and desserts.
Badi elaichiBlack cardamom. Smoky and big, for rich savoury dishes.
LaungClove. Strong and numbing, used whole or ground.
DalchiniCinnamon (cassia) bark. Sweet and warm.
Kali mirchBlack pepper. The sharp heat in many spice mixes.
Lal mirchRed chilli. Dried, for heat and colour.
Hari mirchGreen chilli. Fresh heat.
HingAsafoetida. A pungent resin used in tiny pinches; mellows when cooked.
MethiFenugreek. Seeds are bitter-sweet; leaves are fresh greens.
Kasuri methiDried fenugreek leaves, crumbled over rich gravies.
SarsonMustard. The seeds and oil of north and east Indian cooking.
RaiSmall black mustard seeds that pop in hot oil.
SaunfFennel seeds. Sweet, often chewed after a meal.
AjwainCarom seeds. Sharp like thyme, used in breads and fried snacks.
Tej pattaIndian bay leaf. Cinnamon-like aroma in rice and curries.
JaiphalNutmeg. Used sparingly in sweets and garam masala.
JavitriMace, the lacy covering of nutmeg.
KalonjiNigella seeds. Peppery little black seeds for breads and pickles.
KesarSaffron. The costliest spice; a few threads colour and perfume biryani and sweets.
AmchurDried unripe mango powder, for sourness.
AnardanaDried pomegranate seeds, tangy.
ImliTamarind. Sour pulp for chutneys, sambar and rasam.
Kala namakBlack salt. Sulphurous and savoury, key to chaat.
Chaat masalaA tangy blend with amchur and black salt sprinkled on snacks and fruit.
Garam masalaA warming blend of ground spices, added near the end of cooking.
Sambar powderA South Indian blend of lentils, chillies and spices for sambar.
Panch phoronA Bengali five-seed mix: fenugreek, nigella, mustard, cumin and fennel.
